# Blewbury Beer Festival
## Brand & Design Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft
**Date:** August 2026

---

# Purpose

These guidelines define the visual identity, design language and tone of voice for the Blewbury Beer Festival website and associated promotional material.

The objective is consistency rather than restriction. Every page, poster and future enhancement should feel as though it belongs to the same festival.

---

# Brand Values

The festival exists to:

- Bring the community together.
- Raise money for good causes.
- Celebrate excellent beer and cider.
- Support local businesses where possible.
- Provide a relaxed, welcoming day for everyone.

The website should reinforce these values.

---

# Brand Personality

If the festival were a person it would be:

- Friendly
- Welcoming
- Relaxed
- Community-focused
- Confident
- Well organised
- Authentic
- Unpretentious

The festival should never feel:

- Corporate
- Commercial
- Exclusive
- Flashy
- Trendy for the sake of it
- Over-designed

---

# Emotional Keywords

Every design decision should reinforce one or more of these themes.

- Village
- Community
- Autumn
- Beer
- Craft
- Quality
- Relaxed
- Local

---

# Design Principles

The website should feel like:

- A premium village event
- A beautifully designed festival poster
- Something worth attending

It should **not** feel like:

- A software product
- An e-commerce site
- A corporate brochure
- A generic WordPress theme

---

# Typography

## Headings

Large.

Bold.

Poster-inspired.

Whitespace is encouraged.

## Body

Simple.

Readable.

Short paragraphs.

## Labels

Uppercase.

Small.

Consistent.

Examples:

- SAVE THE DATE
- GOOD CAUSES
- LATEST NEWS
- VISIT

---

# Layout

Use generous whitespace.

Avoid clutter.

Each section should have one purpose.

Prefer fewer, larger components over many small ones.

---

# Homepage Philosophy

The homepage answers only four questions:

1. What is it?
2. When is it?
3. Where is it?
4. Why should I come?

Everything else belongs on dedicated pages.

# Photography

Photography is optional.

The site must not rely on it.

Avoid stock photography.

Preferred imagery:

- Beer pumps
- Glassware
- Barrels
- Hop plants
- Festival signage
- Musicians
- Food

Avoid identifiable crowd photography unless permission has been obtained.

---

# Illustration

Simple.

Graphic.

Modern.

Avoid novelty beer clip-art.

Preferred motifs:

- Hop cones
- Barley
- Pint glasses
- Circular festival graphics

---

# Buttons

Only two styles:

Primary

- Filled
- Burgundy
- Rounded

Secondary

- Outline
- Transparent

No additional button styles.

---

# Animation

Subtle only.

Acceptable:

- Fade
- Hover
- Small movement

Avoid:

- Parallax
- Bounce
- Spin
- Auto-play effects

---

# Tone of Voice

Write like a member of the organising committee.

Never like a marketing department.

Prefer:

"What's on the bar?"

rather than

"Explore our drinks selection"

Keep language:

- Friendly
- Warm
- Natural
- Concise

---

# Accessibility

Accessibility is a design requirement.

Minimum standards:

- Excellent contrast
- Keyboard navigation
- Semantic HTML
- Alt text
- Responsive layouts

---

# Performance

Performance is part of the brand.

Keep pages lightweight.

Avoid unnecessary JavaScript.

Optimise images.

---

# Graphic Motif

Develop a recognisable visual device that can appear consistently across:

- Website
- Posters
- Social media
- Signage
- Volunteer clothing

Potential inspiration:

- Stylised hop cone
- Festival stamp
- Pint viewed from above

---

# Future Expansion

Future features should extend this design language rather than replace it.

---

# Success Criteria

Visitors should immediately understand:

- What the event is
- When it is
- Where it is
- Why they should attend

The site should create anticipation rather than overwhelm.

The overall impression should be:

> "That looks like a really well organised village beer festival."
