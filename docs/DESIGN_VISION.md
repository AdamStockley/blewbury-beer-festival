# Blewbury Beer Festival — Design Vision

## Purpose

This document is the visual and experiential north star for the site.

The aim is not merely to replace WordPress. The aim is to create a digital identity that makes the festival feel welcoming, well organised and worth attending.

## The Desired Reaction

A visitor should think:

> "That looks like a really good village beer festival."

Not:

> "That is a clever website."

## Principles

### Premium, not corporate

The site should feel carefully designed without feeling commercial or expensive.

### Friendly, not cluttered

Information should be easy to find and written in natural language.

### Festival atmosphere without cliché

Avoid generic Oktoberfest imagery, novelty pint cartoons and stock crowd photography.

Use strong typography, warm colour, simple graphics and authentic festival content.

### Poster-led visual identity

Large type, strong dates, restrained colour and simple geometric forms should make the site feel related to a well-designed event poster.

### Photography is optional

The site must look complete without identifiable crowd photography.

Approved product, brewery, performer or venue imagery can be introduced where appropriate.

### Mobile first

A visitor checking the site on the day should be able to find key information quickly on a phone.

### Fast enough to feel instant

Performance is part of the experience.

Avoid unnecessary JavaScript and oversized assets.

### Content over tooling

The festival occurs infrequently. Do not build complex CMS or administration tooling unless a real recurring need appears.

It is acceptable for content changes to be made directly in the repository with assistance.

## Drinks Experience

The public Drinks page is a festival menu, not a stock sheet.

Display:

- Producer / brewery
- Product name
- Style
- ABV
- Short visitor-friendly description
- Optional approved artwork

Do not display:

- cask / keg / pin
- stock volume
- internal sort data
- organiser status fields

Operational information may remain in structured content for organiser use.

## Success Test

If a design decision makes the site:

- harder to scan,
- more corporate,
- more generic,
- slower,
- or more complicated to maintain

without making the festival more appealing or useful to visitors, it is probably the wrong decision.
